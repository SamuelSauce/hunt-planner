Idaho deer Hunt 1045 closes October 31, 2026, after a 27-day any-weapon season across Area 55. Its current numbers explain both the attraction and the work. Idaho Fish and Game recorded 2,384 first-choice applications for 320 awards in the 2026 first drawing, a displayed 13% result. The latest completed mandatory-reporting table lists 215 deer from 301 hunters in 2025, or 71% success.

Those figures do not make the hunt easy. Nearly 48% of the area's surface-management layer is private, no Access Yes! property is currently listed, and more than 4,000 private acres lie within City of Rocks National Reserve. The useful question for a current tag holder is how to turn a favorable recent harvest record into a lawful, repeatable late-October plan.

## The quick verdict

Hunt 1045 best fits a hunter who can spend several days checking broad sage, agricultural-edge and mountain-cover systems while treating every access chain as a separate research problem. The 2025 hunters averaged about 5.2 days afield. That effort figure is a more honest planning anchor than the 71% headline.

For future applicants, this remains a random controlled hunt. Idaho does not award preference points, so an unsuccessful year does not move an applicant into a stronger points tier. The 2026 result describes one completed drawing, not a bankable future probability.

## What the 2026 tag authorizes

IDFG lists Hunt 1045 for antlered mule deer and white-tailed deer from October 5 through October 31, 2026. It covers all of Unit 55, carries 320 tags and allows any legal weapon. For this antlered-only season, a legal deer must have at least one antler longer than three inches, and the antlers must accompany the carcass in transit. The current Hunt Planner page lists no special hunt restriction.

“Any weapon” is not a shortcut around the rest of the regulations. The [2026 Idaho Big Game Seasons and Rules](https://idfg.idaho.gov/sites/default/files/seasons-rules-big-game-2026.pdf) controls legal methods, evidence of sex, transportation and other requirements. Recheck the live hunt page and the brochure before hunting because corrections, closures and emergency changes can outdate a summary.

## The 2026 first draw became slightly more crowded

The official 2026 residency table reports 2,033 resident first choices for 288 awards, a displayed 14% result. It reports 351 nonresident first choices for 32 awards, or 9%. Together that is 2,384 first-choice applicants for 320 awards and a displayed 13% result. Another 1,173 applicants listed the hunt second, but first choices filled the quota.

In 2025, the same first drawing drew 2,319 first choices for 320 awards and displayed 14% overall. The 2024 row lists 2,312 first choices and the same quota. First-choice demand therefore rose by 65 applications, or 2.8%, from 2025 to 2026. That is a real change, but too small and too dependent on applicant behavior to justify a prediction about 2027.

The official page also shows 13 unclaimed tags awarded in a 2026 second drawing. That separate event does not mean the hunt was easy to draw: it lists 802 first-choice applications for those 13 tags, a displayed 2% result. Keep the first and second drawings separate rather than blending them into one odds claim.

## Attach the denominator to 71% success

IDFG's 2025 mandatory-reporting row lists 301 hunters, 215 harvested deer and 1,560 hunter-days. That produces 71% success and about 5.2 days per hunter. In 2024, 308 hunters reported 184 deer and 60% success across 1,638 days. In 2023, 298 hunters reported 152 deer and 51% success across 1,714 days.

The three-year sequence is encouraging, but it is not a controlled experiment. Weather, access, effort, deer distribution and the hunters who actually went afield changed from year to year. It also does not guarantee that the still-open 2026 season will finish like 2025. Use the recent record to justify enough time and multiple backup systems, not to promise an outcome.

## Area 55 is a checkerboard problem before it is a deer problem

The current IDFG surface-management summary estimates Area 55 at 47.64% private, 34.93% Bureau of Land Management, 11.61% Forest Service, 4.56% State of Idaho and 1.25% National Park Service. The agency cautions that the managing agency may not be the same as the owner. It also lists no current Access Yes! property in the hunt area.

That mix makes a public percentage less useful than a complete access chain. Verify the destination parcel, every road and crossing used to reach it, parking, current travel designation and carcass-recovery authority. A public block behind private ground is not automatically accessible, and a road drawn on a basemap is not proof that it is public or open.

The [BLM Burley Field Office](https://www.blm.gov/office/burley-field-office) manages public land in this part of south-central Idaho and maintains current map, advisory and closure links. Use those live sources alongside the [IDFG Hunt Planner Map Center](https://idfg.idaho.gov/ifwis/huntplanner/) rather than treating a static boundary image as a route plan.

## City of Rocks needs its own rule check

IDFG says most of City of Rocks National Reserve is open to hunting, but that sentence is not blanket permission. The [National Park Service hunting page](https://www.nps.gov/ciro/planyourvisit/hunting-and-archery.htm) says motor vehicles must stay on established, authorized roadways and prohibits shooting from or across public highways, campsites, parking lots, structures and access roads. It also notes more than 4,000 acres of private property within the reserve boundary.

The practical implication is to treat the reserve as a distinct planning layer. Confirm the current ownership map, lawful discharge locations, road status and park rules with the visitor center before relying on it. Do not infer access from the reserve outline alone.

## A late-October field plan

IDFG's [2026 Deer and Elk Hunting Outlook](https://idfg.idaho.gov/article/2026-deer-and-elk-hunting-outlook) says the Magic Valley's hot, dry summer could concentrate mule deer near dependable forage and water. That is a regional planning hypothesis, not a location claim for Hunt 1045. Test broad habitat transitions with fresh tracks, droppings, feeding sign and direct observations, then abandon stale assumptions quickly.

- **Recheck the legal frame.** Confirm the live season, boundary, antler definition, weapon rules and carcass requirements.
- **Build two or three complete access systems.** Each should include lawful arrival, parking, foot travel and recovery, not just a public parcel.
- **Separate habitat from ownership.** Productive-looking edges can cross private, BLM, Forest Service, state and NPS management in a short distance.
- **Budget several days.** The 2025 effort record argues against a one-morning expectation even in a high-success year.
- **Keep locations private.** Share habitat patterns and decision logic, never precise camps, private approaches, glassing points, routes or animal coordinates.

Hunt 1045's current story is not simply “71% success.” It is a competitive random tag attached to a long October window, improving recent harvest results and a demanding ownership mosaic. The best use of the evidence is to plan more carefully, not to scout less.
