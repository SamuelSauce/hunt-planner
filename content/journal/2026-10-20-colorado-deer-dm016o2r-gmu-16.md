Colorado buck deer Hunt DM016O2R opens in GMU 16 on October 24, 2026. This brief is for a license holder using the final four days before the opener to decide whether the North Park migration has already changed the hunt, and for a future applicant tempted to call this an easy tag. The draw was accessible in 2026. The latest harvest result was not.

Start with the [static Hunt Planner profile for DM016O2R](/hunts/colorado/deer/dm016o2r-gmu-16/) and [open GMU 16 in the Hunt Planner 3D map](/?state=CO&hunt=DM016O2R&residency=resident&view=3d). The card uses the actual GMU 16 polygon. It is an orientation graphic, not proof of public access, an open road or deer remaining on a particular slope.

## The quick verdict

DM016O2R best fits a current tag holder who can scout several legal sagebrush-to-timber systems immediately before the season and abandon a stale summer-range plan. Colorado Parks and Wildlife's 2026 recap shows that all 23 first-choice general applicants drew, but the 2025 harvest survey estimates only five bucks from 36 hunters, or 13.9% success.

Those facts are not contradictory. The hunt can be accessible in the draw and difficult in the field. CPW's North Park herd plan says most mule deer begin leaving the five-unit herd area as early as October, with the main movements going north toward Wyoming and smaller cohorts moving elsewhere. A second-rifle license that starts October 24 is therefore a migration-and-access problem, not merely a preference-point purchase.

## What the 2026 license covers

The current 2026 Big Game brochure lists `D-M-016-O2-R` as a List A buck deer license valid only in GMU 16 from October 24 through November 1. Do not substitute the October 14–18 first-rifle code, the November 7–15 third-rifle code or the later DAU-wide fourth-rifle code for this boundary and season.

The primary-draw recap lists a 40-license quota and a zero balance after the draw. Its post-draw distribution was 20 resident adults, 12 nonresident adults, two resident youths, four unrestricted landowner-preference licenses and two restricted landowner-preference licenses. A person without a license should not read “all first choices drew” as evidence that a tag remains available now.

## The draw reached choice two

CPW recorded 23 first-choice general applications: 14 resident adults, seven nonresident adults and two resident youths. All 23 were successful. The remaining general inventory reached second choices. At the final adult level, six of 17 resident second-choice applicants and five of 13 nonresident second-choice applicants drew. The two resident youth applicants shown at zero preference points also drew.

That sequence matters more than a quota-to-application shortcut. Forty divided by 23 is not an odds calculation because the quota also served second-choice, youth and landowner paths. For a 2027 applicant, the useful 2026 fact is that the code did not exhaust its general licenses on first choice. CPW warns that quotas, applicant pools and hunt codes change from year to year, so this is history rather than a promise.

## The 2025 harvest estimate was 13.9%

CPW's 2025 deer harvest report estimates five bucks harvested by 36 DM016O2R hunters across 161 recreation days. That is 13.9% success and about 4.5 recreation days per hunter. The report does not mark this row with its low-response asterisk, but the numbers are still survey estimates extrapolated from voluntary responses—not a census and not a prediction for 2026.

The 2025 result also belongs to last year's weather, migration timing, access and hunting pressure. It should temper the idea that a low draw threshold means an easy hunt, but it cannot tell a 2026 hunter which portion of GMU 16 currently holds deer.

## Herd scale is not hunt scale

CPW's 2025 population table estimates 4,830 post-hunt deer and 25 bucks per 100 does in DAU D-03. That herd area combines GMUs 6, 16, 17, 161 and 171. Neither figure is a GMU 16 count, and neither measures the number of bucks legally reachable during this nine-day season.

The agency's January 2023 North Park plan supplies the more useful landscape context. Across the full 1.036-million-acre DAU, it reported 35.9% private land, 31.9% Forest Service, 18.2% Bureau of Land Management, 12% state land and 1.7% Arapaho National Wildlife Refuge. Those are herd-area ownership shares, not GMU 16 access percentages. Public color on a map still requires legal ingress, current motor-vehicle rules and any property-specific restriction.

The same plan describes higher summer concentrations near sagebrush and aspen-conifer interfaces around 8,000 to 10,000 feet, then says migration can begin in early October. Use that as a broad search model: compare legal systems that connect remaining forage, transition cover and travel terrain. Do not treat the elevation band or a historic corridor as a waypoint.

## A four-day preparation plan

- **Audit the exact boundary.** Confirm the GMU 16 line, parcel ownership, legal entry, parking and motorized-route status. Build at least two independent access plans rather than two approaches that depend on the same private crossing.
- **Replace summer assumptions with current evidence.** Glass broad sagebrush-to-aspen or conifer transitions, then let current tracks, droppings and direct observations decide where to spend time. A quiet basin with old sign may simply be behind the migration.
- **Plan for shared pressure.** Second rifle puts deer, elk and bear hunters in the field at the same time. Give other hunters room, keep a safe shooting plan and expect movement to change after the opener.
- **Prepare recovery before the shot.** Check the carry, vehicle route, tagging steps and cooling plan in advance. Open country can compress distance visually while private boundaries complicate retrieval.
- **Make a CWD decision now.** CPW says no deer hunt is subject to mandatory testing in 2026; voluntary deer testing costs $25. Decide whether to test and review carcass-transport rules before leaving home.

Keep all shared guidance at the habitat-pattern level. Do not publish camps, private approaches, migration crossings, bedding areas, glassing points or animal coordinates.

## Verification checklist

1. Match the physical license to `DM016O2R`, buck deer, GMU 16 and October 24–November 1, 2026.
2. Recheck legal methods, fluorescent orange or pink requirements, shooting hours, tagging and evidence-of-sex rules in the current brochure.
3. Verify every ownership edge, road, gate, closure and retrieval route with CPW and the responsible land manager.
4. Keep the 2025 harvest survey, 2025 five-unit herd estimate and 2026 draw in their correct evidence years.
5. Treat 13.9% as a historical survey estimate and the choice-two draw as one year's applicant sequence.
6. Keep scouting and discussion general enough to protect current animal locations and lawful access relationships.
