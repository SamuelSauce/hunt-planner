Utah Manti/San Rafael any-legal-weapon deer Hunt DB1533 opens October 17, 2026, with the largest current permit headline among Utah general-season deer hunts not already covered in this journal. Hunt Planner's current record lists 5,400 permits. The completed 2026 draw recorded 9,085 adult resident participants and 3,438 successes, while Utah's preliminary 2025 harvest report recorded 1,921 bucks from 4,060 hunters afield, or 47.3% success. Demand, permits and field results are three different questions.

Start with the [static Hunt Planner profile for DB1533](/hunts/utah/deer/db1533-manti-san-rafael/) and [open DB1533 in the Hunt Planner 3D map](/?state=UT&hunt=DB1533&residency=resident&view=3d). The card above uses the actual hunt boundary. It is an orientation graphic, not an ownership layer, route map or legal boundary substitute.

## The quick verdict

DB1533 best fits a hunter who already holds the permit, can verify several independent access plans and is prepared to use most of the nine-day season. The 2025 group averaged four days afield, and slightly more than half of hunters reported no harvest. A large permit number and a growing unit-scale deer estimate do not remove the work of finding legal access and current deer use after opening pressure arrives.

For a future applicant, the 2026 adult resident result was not a zero-point opportunity in the ordinary sense. Nearly all two-point applicants succeeded, but fewer than half of one-point participants did. The displayed zero-point successes were also classified as lifetime-license successes. Read the point rows before reducing this hunt to a single odds percentage.

## The 2026 draw concentrated at one and two points

Utah's completed 2026 portal lists 9,085 adult resident participants and 3,438 successes for DB1533. At two points, 1,620 of 1,637 participants succeeded. At one point, 1,303 of 3,026 succeeded. The portal shows 197 successes among 4,103 zero-point participants, but all 197 are simultaneously identified in its lifetime-license field. That row should not be presented as a regular 4.8% zero-point chance.

Adult nonresidents had a more accessible result: 373 successes among 511 participants. All 17 participants at two points succeeded, 151 of 157 succeeded at one point and 175 of 307 succeeded at zero. Thirteen of those zero-point successes are identified as lifetime-license successes. Utah also displays 1,227 resident youth participants and nine nonresident youth participants for DB1533, with every participant in those youth rows successful.

The 2025 adult draw was slightly tighter. Utah's historical report lists 8,419 resident applicants and 2,951 permits: 1,572 of 1,587 succeeded at two points, 1,066 of 2,964 at one, and none of 3,551 at zero. Nonresidents recorded 328 permits among 573 applicants. The comparison shows more 2026 participation and more adult successes, but it does not create a forecast for 2027.

Keep the public permit fields separate. The current DB1533 profile lists 5,400 permits, while the 2026 draw portal displays 5,047 total successes across its adult and youth rows. Utah's unit table lists 9,000 permits for the full Manti/San Rafael general-season allocation across weapon seasons. Those records describe different scopes, and the public pages do not assign every difference. Combining them into one crowd estimate would be speculation.

## The 2025 result is useful context, not a personal probability

Utah's preliminary 2025 harvest report lists 4,507 DB1533 permits, 4,060 hunters afield, 1,921 bucks, 47.3% success, four average days and 3.5 average satisfaction. Hunt Planner's current 2026 permit headline is 893 higher than the 2025 report's permit count, a 19.8% increase between those two fields.

That comparison does not prove that 893 additional hunters will be afield at once, or that success must fall. The harvest survey does not separate public- and private-land results, identify productive dates or measure where pressure displaced deer. Its practical message is narrower: last year's group used almost half the season on average, and 2,139 hunters reported no buck.

The unit-scale management evidence helps explain why opportunity grew. Utah's April 2026 permit table lists a 2025 Manti/San Rafael estimate of 32,400 deer against a 38,000 objective, up from 28,400 in 2024. It reports 21.5 bucks per 100 does in 2025 and a 19.8 three-year average against a 15–17 objective. The same table increased the full unit's general-season allocation from 8,300 permits in 2025 to 9,000 in 2026. These are management-scale measures, not a count of deer inside DB1533 on opening morning.

## Treat Manti/San Rafael as several access problems

Utah's 2018 unit plan, revised in 2020, describes a large landscape spanning mountain summer range, steep plateau margins, sagebrush and pinyon-juniper winter habitat, lower desert country and agricultural corridors. Its ownership table also changes sharply by seasonal range: Forest Service dominates the mapped summer range, while private, Forest Service, BLM and state lands all make up meaningful portions of mapped winter range. Those percentages are not unitwide public-land percentages and do not prove legal ingress to any parcel.

That variety argues for separate plans rather than a long list of nearby pins:

- Compare broad feed-to-cover transitions in more than one terrain type, then let fresh tracks, sightings and lawful access determine where to spend time.
- Validate ownership and legal ingress before scoring habitat. Public land beyond a private parcel is not automatically reachable.
- Check the current Forest Service Motor Vehicle Use Map, BLM route designations, gates, wildfire information and temporary closures immediately before travel. The San Rafael Swell's BLM travel network changed under a December 2024 decision, so an old track is not evidence of current motorized access.
- Expect opening pressure to alter deer visibility. Keep alternatives reached through genuinely different access systems instead of several options dependent on the same road.
- Plan tagging, recovery and cooling before taking a shot; never assume a vehicle route or private crossing will be available after the fact.
- Keep shared scouting information general. Do not publish camps, private approaches, water sources, bedding cover, glassing points, routes or animal coordinates.

## What to verify before October 17

1. Match the permit to DB1533, Manti/San Rafael, buck deer, any legal weapon and October 17–25, 2026.
2. Carry the current agency boundary and confirm ownership and lawful entry independently of the article card.
3. Read the current 2026 field regulations for weapon, hunter-orange, tagging, evidence-of-sex, vehicle and harvest-reporting rules.
4. Recheck land-manager closures, wildfire effects, weather, gates and road conditions close to departure.
5. Keep the current 5,400-permit profile, 5,047 completed draw successes, preliminary 2025 harvest and unitwide 9,000-permit allocation separate.
6. Treat draw and harvest history as context, never as a promise about weather, pressure, deer distribution or personal success.
