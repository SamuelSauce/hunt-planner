Utah deer Hunt DB1009 opens today, October 26, 2026, for five days on the Henry Mountains. Its 11 permit holders arrive with an unusually strong historical signal: Utah's preliminary 2025 report recorded 11 hunters, 11 management bucks and 100% success in 2.2 average days.

That result does **not** make this an any-buck hunt. A legal management buck has three points or fewer on at least one antler above and including the first fork. The eye guard does not count, but a damaged or broken projection longer than one inch does. The hunt's first job is therefore accurate classification, not finding the largest silhouette on a famous premium unit.

## The quick verdict

DB1009 best fits a permit holder who can slow the encounter down, inspect both antlers from more than one angle and walk away whenever cover, distance or movement hides a tine. Last year's 100% result is useful preparation evidence, but it is not permission to rush a legal determination.

The 2026 season follows immediately after the October 17–25 Henry Mountains premium any-legal-weapon hunt. A practical opening-day plan should assume that some deer have already reacted to nine days of vehicle traffic and glassing pressure. Compare several broad habitat systems instead of treating an old sighting or road as a fixed destination.

## What “management buck” means in the field

Utah's 2026 field guide defines the legal animal by each antler, not by total points or an average between the two sides. One antler must have no more than three qualifying points above and including the first fork. The other antler can carry more. The eye guard is excluded from the count; a broken or damaged point still counts when its projection is longer than one inch from base to tip.

That structure creates a real identification problem. A buck can look heavy, old or asymmetrical and still qualify, while a smaller-looking buck can be illegal if both antlers carry too many qualifying points. Body size, antler mass and reputation of the unit are not substitutes for the rule.

A conservative sequence is simple:

- Get a stable view of both antlers and locate each first fork.
- Count each antler separately, excluding the eye guard but including qualifying broken projections.
- Change the viewing angle when a beam, ear or vegetation can hide a point.
- Do not shoot if either side needed to make the buck legal is not clearly resolved.

Utah also makes the consequence explicit: a management-buck permit holder may not retain a buck that fails the definition. The 2026 permit is the hunter's only buck deer permit for the year, uses the hunter's deer bonus points and brings a five-year waiting period.

## Eleven permits, unchanged from 2025

The 2026 permit table assigns 10 resident and one nonresident permit to DB1009, the same 11-permit structure approved for 2025. The current Henry Mountains unit plan sets management-buck permits at 10–20% of total premium limited-entry permits and says the purpose is to help move the buck-to-doe ratio toward the unit objective.

This is selective population management, not a lesser version of the premium hunt. The unit plan separately tracks premium buck age structure, management-buck harvest, herd composition, survival and modeled population. It also warns that additional strategies may be needed to raise management-buck harvest. A successful hunter is participating in that defined objective, which makes accurate antler identification and mandatory reporting part of the management result.

## The 2025 draw was part points, part random chance

In the 2025 draw, 198 residents competed for 10 permits, a total ratio of 1 in 19.8. Five resident permits went through the highest-bonus-point share and five through the random share. The random permits reached applicants at 15, 14, 7, 6 and 4 points, while several higher point tiers drew none. That is why the overall ratio and lowest successful point should not be presented as a cutoff.

The lone 2025 nonresident permit came from 102 eligible applicants, an overall ratio of 1 in 102. The successful applicant held eight points, but that one result is not a forecast. Utah's limited-entry system reserves half the permits for applicants with the greatest bonus-point totals and returns the other permits and unsuccessful applicants to the random drawing. Every applicant receives random numbers tied to the application and accumulated bonus points.

These are 2025 outcomes, not 2026 odds and not a prediction for the next application year. The quota is current for 2026; the draw evidence is one year older.

## Read 100% beside the sample size

The preliminary 2025 harvest table reports 11 permits, 11 hunters afield, 11 harvested bucks, 100% success, 2.2 average days and 4.6 average satisfaction on a five-point scale. All six figures belong together. The result says a very small group found legal animals efficiently under last year's conditions; it does not estimate the probability for one hunter this week.

The management hunt also followed a 2025 premium season that harvested 26 bucks from 26 hunters afield. Those are different permits with different legal-animal rules. Do not blend the two 100% figures into one forecast or use premium-hunt antler expectations to judge a DB1009 target.

## A five-day, pressure-aware plan

- **Build independent access options.** The Bureau of Land Management finalized a Henry Mountains/Fremont Gorge travel plan that keeps many major routes open and retains some seasonal big-game access in Wilderness Study Areas. That does not make every visible two-track legal or passable. Check the current travel map, closures, weather and road surface before committing a vehicle.
- **Work at the habitat-pattern scale.** The 2025 unit plan emphasizes sagebrush-steppe, pinyon-juniper treatments, winter range and riparian cover. Compare broad feed-to-cover transitions and let current tracks and observations decide where to stay. This article intentionally provides no camps, glassing points, travel routes or coordinates.
- **Expect displaced deer.** The premium rifle season ended yesterday. Use the first observation period to test whether traffic has moved deer away from exposed openings or easy road overlooks, then adjust without forcing entry through private land or closed routes.
- **Leave time for classification.** The best-looking final-minute opportunity is still wrong if the antler that makes the buck legal is obscured. Build enough schedule margin to observe, recover and care for meat without letting the five-day season become a countdown.
- **Follow current technology rules.** Utah prohibits trail cameras on public land from July 31 through December 31 and prohibits possessing night-vision devices while locating or taking big game in that period.
- **Report the result.** Complete the mandatory harvest survey within 30 days after the October 30 close, successful or not.

DB1009 is timely because its season begins today, but its durable lesson is precision. Eleven hunters filled 11 permits in 2025, yet the number that matters before any shot is three or fewer on at least one qualifying antler. Verify the map, verify the route and verify the antlers independently.
