Wyoming elk Hunt 113-1 opens today, November 5, 2026, with a combination that rarely appears in the same record: a scarce any-elk license, strong recent harvest and substantial public land that the agency calls accessible. The final regulation authorizes 50 Type 1 licenses through November 30. The completed 2026 resident report lists 741 first-choice applicants for 18 licenses in that draw table. In 2025, Wyoming Game and Fish Department estimated 42 harvested elk from 53 active Type 1 hunters, or 79.2% success.

Those numbers make Area 113 attractive, but they need labels. The 50-license regulation quota is not the resident draw allocation. The 79.2% result is a survey estimate from the prior season, not a 2026 forecast. And 65% public land does not excuse a hunter from checking the ownership and travel status of every approach.

The card above uses the actual Area 113 boundary. It does not establish a legal route, road opening or permission.

## The quick verdict

Hunt 113-1 best fits a tag holder who wants a late prairie-elk hunt and is willing to search rolling sagebrush, broken ridges and ponderosa cover rather than wait for a classic high-country pattern. WGFD says Area 113 provides unusually good public-land elk access for this part of Wyoming, and its 2026 management report calls it a coveted public-land area. That is meaningful—but so is the page's “Difficult Public Access” warning.

The hunt is a poor fit for someone treating the 79.2% success rate as a trophy-bull forecast. The Type 1 license is valid for any elk. WGFD's Hunt Planner explicitly advises hunters to temper trophy expectations even though the area is managed for mature-bull ratios and high success.

## What the 2026 Type 1 license authorizes

The final 2026 Chapter 7 regulation lists Area 113 Type 1 as any elk, with a 50-license quota. Its special-archery season ran September 1-30; the regular season runs November 5-30. Area 113 also has Type 3 and Type 8 licenses, but their limitations are different. Type 3 is spike or antlerless elk through December 31. Type 8 is unlimited cow-or-calf authority on private land through January 31.

Do not borrow those later dates or animal limitations for a Type 1 plan. The Type 8 license is particularly relevant to the herd-management story, but it does not turn its private-land authority into access for a Type 1 holder.

## Resident demand grew, but the allocation grew faster

WGFD's June 15, 2026 resident demand report lists 18 licenses and 741 first-choice applicants for 113-1. That is a raw quota-to-first-choice ratio of 2.43%. Hunt Planner's local 2025 record lists 14 licenses and 693 first choices, or 2.02%.

First-choice demand increased by 48 applicants, 6.9%, while the reported resident allocation increased by four licenses, 28.6%. The raw ratio therefore improved by about 0.41 percentage point. That is a completed-draw comparison, not an individual probability or a promise about 2027. Party applications, allocations and applicant behavior can change the result.

The regulation's 50-license quota and the resident report's 18 licenses answer different questions. Adding draw-table supplies across resident, landowner and nonresident pools—or calling 50 the resident supply—would misstate Wyoming's system.

## No nonresident random licenses in 2026

Nonresidents also need four separate results. In the 2026 regular preference report, three Hunt 113-1 licenses were available: two went to two first-choice applicants at 20 points, and the final license went to one of three at 19 points. None went below 19. The special preference report shows two licenses: one went at 19 points and one at 17, with none below 17.

Neither 2026 random report offered a Hunt 113-1 license. The regular random table still recorded 144 first choices, and the special random table recorded 74. Those applicants did not face tiny positive random odds; the reported random quota was zero in both pools.

Paying for the special pool changed the preference competition, not the hunt itself, and it did not create a 2026 random tag. Keep regular preference, special preference, regular random and special random results separate.

## Read 79.2% beside the sample and workload

WGFD's 2025 Hunt Planner report lists 390 hunter-days, 42 harvested elk and 79.2% Type 1 success. The local structured record identifies 53 active hunters. The result is strong, but the sample is small and the any-elk license does not isolate mature-bull success.

The current Sheridan Region management report adds one useful detail: WGFD estimated that the 42 Type 1 elk harvested in Area 113 during 2025 were mature bulls. It also reports 2025 Type 3 success of 87% and Type 8 success of 82%, but those are different licenses with different animal and land limitations. They should not be folded into the Type 1 rate.

The same management report describes the Rochelle Hills herd—Areas 113 and 123 together—as 133% above its trend-based objective in 2025. Managers counted 1,867 elk against an 800-animal trend objective, while warning that trend counts are snapshots affected by conditions and distribution. The report proposes a 1,500 count for 2026 and says the season structure is intended to address a herd over objective. That is management context, not an Area 113 density guarantee.

WGFD's statewide 2026 forecast is directionally consistent: Casper-region elk populations remain above objective, and the agency expects good success for Type 1 and 2 any-elk hunters. The forecast does not publish a Hunt 113-1 success prediction.

## Public-land opportunity still needs an access audit

WGFD estimates Area 113 at 65% public land, rates access “Good” and highlights the Thunder Basin National Grassland. It describes the hunt as unusually accessible for this part of the state. Yet the official page also carries a difficult-public-access warning. Both facts belong in the plan.

The country is a prairie-elk system: rolling sagebrush hills interrupted by rocky ridges, with ponderosa pine on higher ground. Elk may use any of those habitat types. That favors a broad habitat-pattern search—security cover, broken relief, current forage, pressure and fresh sign—rather than a published camp, route, glassing point or coordinate.

Verify the entire access chain before leaving a maintained road: parcel status, the legal approach, current travel rules, parking authority, foot access and retrieval. Public land beyond a private road is not automatically reachable. A line on an old map does not prove a route is open. Keep at least one independent public-land alternative, and check current weather and road conditions before committing to soft surfaces.

## An opener plan without spot burning

- **Prove access first.** Resolve ownership, route status, gates, parking and retrieval while service is available.
- **Search the prairie as elk habitat.** Compare rolling sage, broken rock and timbered ridge patterns, then let fresh tracks, forage and pressure update the plan.
- **Budget for a full 26-day window.** Strong 2025 success does not mean elk will remain in the first visible basin after opening pressure.
- **Keep license types separate.** Type 1 is any elk through November 30; Type 3 and private-land Type 8 rules do not transfer.
- **Plan meat care and recovery.** Open prairie can make animals visible, but distance, mud, wind and limited vehicle authority can still make retrieval slow.

## Verification checklist

- Match the license to Area 113, Type 1 and the 2026 any-elk limitation.
- Confirm the November 5-30 regular season and 50-license quota in the final 2026 regulation.
- Recheck land status, road authority, closures, fire restrictions and any permission immediately before entry.
- Review tagging, evidence-of-sex, carcass transport and current disease guidance before the shot.
- Keep the 2026 season and draw evidence separate from the 2025 harvest and herd-management evidence.

Hunt 113-1 is difficult to draw because it offers a real combination: late any-elk timing, a public-land majority and strong recent harvest in a prairie system. Its lesson is not that 79.2% makes the outcome easy. It is that good access and an above-objective herd can coexist with a scarce license, a small harvest sample and route-by-route access work.
