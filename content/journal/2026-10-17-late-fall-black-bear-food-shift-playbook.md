This guide is for a Colorado bear hunter trying to answer a late-fall question that a point total cannot: **when a rifle license shares the calendar with deer and elk seasons, which hunt best fits the way bears use food, cover and pressured country?**

The comparison matters on October 17, 2026 because the first concurrent rifle window is open through October 18. The same licenses reopen October 24–November 1, November 7–15 and November 18–22. More calendar does not mean one continuous 40-day bear season. Each break can change natural food, snow, road access, hunter traffic and whether a useful observation area still functions.

The local records are [BE001O5R in GMU 1](/hunts/colorado/bear/be001o5r-gmu-1/), [BE020O5R in GMU 20](/hunts/colorado/bear/be020o5r-gmu-20/) and [BE061O5R in GMU 61](/hunts/colorado/bear/be061o5r-gmu-61/). Every section also links the hunt directly into Hunt Planner's 3D map.

Hunt Planner's structured controlled-hunt bear data are Colorado-only, so there is no defensible two-state comparison to make. This playbook compares three supported Colorado hunts rather than importing unlike general-season records from another state. The card above represents **lead hunt BE001O5R only**, not the full comparison.

## Start with food, then test security and visibility

Colorado Parks and Wildlife says black bears enter hyperphagia in mid-August and shift toward calorie-rich fruits and nuts. The agency identifies gambel oak, serviceberry, chokecherry, hawthorn and pinyon pine among important fall foods, and says bears may move 20–30 miles from summer range to traditional fall range. That is a behavior pattern, not a promise that one slope, crop or elevation is productive today.

Build a late-fall search around four connected questions:

1. **What food is actually present now?** Check broad mast and fruit-producing habitat for current production and fresh sign. A habitat label on a map cannot tell you whether the 2026 crop survived frost, drought or local browsing.
2. **Where is secure cover close enough to use?** Bears need not remain in the open food. Compare brush, timber and broken drainages that let an animal move between feeding and security without crossing a large exposed gap.
3. **Can you observe without forcing a brush push?** CPW recommends locating food and sign, then watching from a higher point. Slow movement through dense brush reduces identification time and adds risk during overlapping rifle seasons.
4. **Can you recover and cool the animal legally?** The useful side of a canyon is the one with lawful entry and a realistic pack-out. A visible bear beyond private land, a park boundary or a closed route is not an opportunity.

The calendar changes that model. Warm afternoons increase meat-care urgency. Snow can expose tracks and concentrate remaining food, but it can also close a road, shorten glassing time or make a steep return unsafe. By the fourth rifle window, some bears may be less active or denning; no date guarantees movement.

## Read the evidence without manufacturing odds

All three are List B, either-sex, limited rifle licenses valid during the four regular 2026 rifle windows. Colorado's preference-point draw is ordered by residency and applicant category, so a raw licenses-to-first-choices division is not a personal draw probability. A “drawn out at” label identifies the final tier reached in one year, not next year's guaranteed threshold.

The same 2026 fee snapshot applies to all three: CPW lists the adult resident bear license at $64.01 and the adult nonresident bear/fishing combination at $302.56. Primary-draw applicants also faced an $8.93 resident or $11.49 nonresident processing fee, a qualifying-license requirement and, for most adults, a $12.76 habitat stamp. Those are license components, not a total hunt budget.

CPW's local static records do not attach harvest success to these hunt codes. The newest official management summary instead publishes **2023–2025 average harvest by bear data analysis unit (DAU)**. A DAU contains several GMUs, methods and seasons, so those totals are useful scale and management context—not BE001O5R, BE020O5R or BE061O5R success rates. Keeping that limitation visible is more honest than borrowing an unrelated percentage.

## Hunt 1: BE001O5R — five licenses and a zero-point final tier

- **Current season and weapon:** Either-sex rifle bear in GMU 1 during October 14–18, October 24–November 1, November 7–15 and November 18–22, 2026. CPW lists it as a List B license.
- **Tag path and 2026 draw demand:** The 2026 primary recap shows five licenses and six first-choice applications. Four resident adults drew; the final adult-resident tier was zero preference points, where four of five drew. One resident youth at zero points also drew. “Zero points” therefore describes the last 2026 tier, not automatic access.
- **Latest harvest context:** DAU 15 includes GMUs 1, 2, 3, 10, 11, 201, 211 and 301. CPW reports an 11-bear average annual harvest for 2023–2025 and an 11-bear average total mortality, with a stable management goal. Those numbers cannot isolate GMU 1 or this five-license code.
- **Public-land and access pattern:** The hunt boundary includes a large northwest Colorado landscape with public management, private inholdings and major non-hunting exclusions nearby. Dinosaur National Monument prohibits hunting. Use CPW's ownership layer and current BLM travel information, then verify the legal route rather than treating a public-colored destination as proof of ingress.
- **Terrain character:** Broad sage and open benches transition into brushy draws, pinyon-juniper breaks and canyon terrain. The field problem is finding a productive food-and-cover edge that can still be watched safely after deer and elk traffic changes the unit.
- **Principal tradeoff:** BE001O5R offered the lightest first-choice demand here, but only five licenses and a large, access-sensitive search area. A favorable 2026 tier does not create hunt-specific harvest evidence.
- **Best for:** A hunter who values low historical point cost, can verify several public approaches and will let present food—not the scarcity of the tag—choose the observation area.

## Hunt 2: BE020O5R — more licenses across three GMUs

- **Current season and weapon:** Either-sex rifle bear during the same four 2026 windows. The official license covers GMUs 20, 29 and 38; the local static profile and linked 3D view are centered on the GMU 20 record.
- **Tag path and 2026 draw demand:** CPW's primary recap lists 135 licenses, 120 first-choice applications and 86 second-choice applications. The adult-resident draw reached second choice, where 14 of 79 at that final level drew; one of four adult nonresidents at second choice drew. Nine resident youths at zero points drew. Those pools should not be collapsed into one odds percentage.
- **Latest harvest context:** DAU 3 includes GMUs 7, 8, 9, 19, 20, 29, 38 and 191. The 2026 management summary gives a 2023–2025 average annual harvest of 31 bears and average total mortality of 60, under a stable goal. It does not separate the three GMUs authorized by BE020O5R or isolate concurrent-rifle harvest.
- **Public-land and access pattern:** The code spans a Front Range mosaic rather than one uniform block. Development, private parcels and heavily used public land can compress legal access. Rocky Mountain National Park prohibits hunting, and a state GMU boundary does not override that exclusion. Verify the exact hunt-code authorization, park line, ownership and motorized route for every plan.
- **Terrain character:** Foothill shrub communities, forested slopes, steep drainages and higher montane country can all occur inside the code's three-GMU footprint. The useful comparison is not “high versus low”; it is current natural food joined to secure cover and a safe observation angle.
- **Principal tradeoff:** BE020O5R supplied 27 times as many 2026 licenses as BE001O5R and reached second choice, but it also spans three GMUs and a complex recreation-and-private-land interface. Calendar flexibility can be consumed by access verification and pressure.
- **Best for:** A hunter prioritizing draw accessibility and multiple broad habitat options who is disciplined enough to treat each GMU as a separate access project.

## Hunt 3: BE061O5R — a high-point Uncompahgre test

- **Current season and weapon:** Either-sex rifle bear in GMU 61 during the four October–November 2026 windows, also as a List B license.
- **Tag path and 2026 draw demand:** CPW allocated 10 licenses. The recap records 138 first-choice applications, 42 second choices, 17 third choices and nine fourth choices. The resident-adult draw stopped at seven preference points, where one of seven drew; the report lists no nonresident or youth licenses drawn. That one-in-seven final tier is conditional on reaching it, not overall odds.
- **Latest harvest context:** DAU 5 includes GMUs 40, 60, 61, 62, 64, 65 and 70. CPW reports a 2023–2025 average annual harvest of 189 bears and average total mortality of 216, with a decrease management goal. The much larger total reflects a large DAU with many licenses, seasons and GMUs; it is not a BE061O5R success claim.
- **Public-land and access pattern:** CPW's Uncompahgre plan describes the broader DAU as 41% BLM, 31% private, 25% Forest Service, 2% state and 1% National Park Service. That older planning breakdown is not a current GMU 61 accessible-acre calculation. Use the Hunting Atlas plus current BLM and Forest Service travel rules, and confirm any private crossing.
- **Terrain character:** The Uncompahgre Plateau combines mesas, oakbrush and serviceberry country, timbered cover and abrupt drainages. Mast failure or heavy pressure can shift the useful habitat quickly, so build several food-cover observation systems rather than one remembered opening.
- **Principal tradeoff:** BE061O5R had by far the steepest 2026 demand and only 10 licenses. In return, it places a hunter in a DAU where current management evidence confirms substantial bear harvest at the broad scale—but not a hunt-code success rate.
- **Best for:** A hunter already invested in Colorado bear points who can scout mast conditions, solve mixed ownership and avoid treating DAU-scale harvest as a guarantee for GMU 61.

## A practical late-fall sequence

First, mark three broad food-cover systems per authorized GMU. Each needs legal entry, observation space, a wind option and a recovery route. Do not publish or circulate exact food patches, beds, camps, private approaches or coordinates.

Second, recheck each system before every rifle window. Record only transferable conditions: whether mast or fruit is present, whether sign is current, how snow changed access and whether hunter pressure removed the observation advantage. If food is gone, change systems instead of extending an old assumption.

Third, identify before preparing to shoot. CPW prohibits taking a cub or an adult accompanied by cubs. Observe small bears for several minutes with binoculars; temporary separation does not prove that a cub is alone. During firearm seasons, wear the required solid fluorescent orange or pink garment and head covering.

Finally, treat recovery as part of selection. Colorado requires edible meat to be cared for and a harvested bear to be presented for inspection within five business days. Decide how the hide, head and meat will leave the terrain before moving from observation to pursuit.

## Best fit by hunter priority

- **For the lightest 2026 first-choice demand:** BE001O5R had six first choices for five licenses and reached zero resident points. Its small quota and missing hunt-level harvest result still argue against calling it “easy.”
- **For the most draw flexibility:** BE020O5R reached second choice with 135 licenses. It best fits someone willing to manage a three-GMU authorization and complicated public-private-recreation boundaries.
- **For an established point holder:** BE061O5R reached seven resident points and had 138 first choices for 10 licenses. It is the clear continuity choice only if the hunter also wants the Uncompahgre access and habitat problem.
- **For the strongest broad harvest scale:** DAU 5's 2023–2025 average harvest was 189, versus 31 in DAU 3 and 11 in DAU 15. Those are differently sized multi-GMU management totals, not a ranking of these licenses.
- **For a lower-complexity field plan:** None wins automatically. GMU 1 has large-scale travel and monument/private boundaries, BE020O5R spans three units, and GMU 61 mixes extensive public management with meaningful private land and high demand.

There is no universal winner. BE001O5R best fits a low-point applicant who can solve a large northwest unit. BE020O5R best fits an opportunity-minded hunter comfortable rebuilding the plan across three GMUs. BE061O5R best fits a point-invested hunter who wants an Uncompahgre food-and-cover problem and understands that broad DAU harvest is not personal success evidence.

## Verification checklist before hunting or applying

1. Confirm the physical license, hunt code, authorized GMUs, List B status, sex, legal method and all four 2026 date windows in the current CPW brochure.
2. Keep the 2026 draw recap separate from 2023–2025 DAU harvest averages. Neither predicts an individual outcome.
3. Recheck current food, weather, fire restrictions, closures, road conditions and daylight before every season segment.
4. Verify ownership, lawful ingress, park and monument exclusions, and current BLM, Forest Service or county travel rules. A public parcel does not guarantee a public route.
5. Use binoculars long enough to rule out a cub or an adult accompanied by cubs. Follow firearm-season orange or pink requirements.
6. Prepare meat care and CPW's mandatory inspection within five business days before entering the field.
7. Keep all terrain advice at habitat-pattern level; do not share exact food sources, camps, routes, glassing points or animal locations.
