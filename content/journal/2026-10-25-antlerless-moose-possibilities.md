This guide is for a hunter who wants to compare **antlerless moose tags as serious meat hunts, not as easier substitutes for bull moose**. Wyoming 038-4, Wyoming 001-4 and Idaho 3094 all authorize an antlerless animal, but the similarities end there. Their seasons, point systems, public-land patterns and sample sizes produce three different planning problems.

The comparison matters on October 25, 2026 because all three official seasons are open now: [Wyoming 038-4, Hunt Area 38](/hunts/wyoming/moose/038-4-hunt-area-38/), [Wyoming 001-4, Hunt Area 1](/hunts/wyoming/moose/001-4-hunt-area-1/) and [Idaho 3094, Area 54](/hunts/idaho/moose/3094-area-54/). The 2026 draws are over, so this is useful in two ways: current tag holders can pressure-test their field plan, and future applicants can see what the newest season, demand and harvest evidence actually says before choosing a draw strategy.

The card above represents **lead hunt Wyoming 038-4 only**, not the full comparison. Every hunt below exists in Hunt Planner's local structured datasets and links to its own profile and 3D map.

## What late fall changes for a cow-moose hunt

By late October the September breeding period is behind most of these hunts. The useful field question is no longer where rutting bulls might advertise; it is where an antlerless animal can meet feed, cover, water and security while roads and daylight become less forgiving. Broad willow-riparian strips, aspen edges, mountain openings and adjacent forest can all matter. In Idaho Area 54, irrigated ground and rangeland add a different edge pattern to the forest component.

Legal-animal identification belongs ahead of shot opportunity. Wyoming Type 4 licenses exclude a cow with a calf at side. Idaho defines an antlerless moose as one without antlers or with antlers less than six inches long. A group can remain partly screened by willows or timber, and a calf may not stand beside the cow when first seen. Slow observation from several angles is a safety and legality step, not lost hunting time.

Weather can increase track visibility and pull use toward exposed forage, but it can also erase a road plan. Snow is evidence about current movement, not proof that moose have entered a particular drainage. The practical late-season pattern is to prepare several lawful habitat systems at different elevations, then let repeated fresh sign, visibility and road condition choose among them.

## Keep the three tag paths separate

Idaho uses a random controlled-hunt draw and does not bank preference points. Wyoming divides moose licenses between preference and random pools, with separate resident and nonresident results. A successful Wyoming Type 4 hunter must then wait five years before applying for or receiving another Type 4 license. None of those rules can be reduced to one blended “draw odds” number.

This guide also does not rank current costs. A defensible total would have to include residency, required licenses or stamps, application and processing charges, refund rules and any point purchase; the comparison has not independently verified the complete 2026 bundle for both states. Use the linked official licensing pages before spending money.

## Hunt 1: Wyoming 038-4 — the long window and largest quota

- **Current season and weapon:** Type 4 antlerless moose, any legal weapon, October 1–November 30, 2026, after a September special-archery season. A cow with a calf at side is not legal. The final regulation authorizes 35 licenses, and the current allocation lists 31 resident and four nonresident licenses.
- **Tag path and 2026 draw demand:** Wyoming preference-random draw. In the resident preference pool, 24 licenses were available; the last partly successful tier was 10 points, where one of nine first-choice applicants drew, while applicants below 10 drew none. The resident random pool listed seven licenses and 259 first choices, a raw 2.7 licenses per 100 first choices. Nonresidents had three preference licenses, with two issued at 21 points, plus one random license for 12 first choices. Those four pools are not interchangeable.
- **Latest harvest result:** Wyoming's 2025 report lists 30 hunters, 22 moose, 73.3% success and 10.5 average days. That estimated survey result describes a long average effort despite a high success headline.
- **Public-land and access pattern:** The local Hunt Planner record estimates 45% public land. That is useful orientation, not proof of legal ingress. Public parcels, open roads, private crossings and current Access Yes rules must form one complete route before a hunter commits to a drainage.
- **Terrain character:** Area 38 is part of the Snowy Range herd landscape in southeastern Wyoming. Agency material points broadly to Pole Mountain, the Snowy Range and the Sierra Madre, with aspen and riparian habitat important to moose. The 2026 regional forecast also notes improving habitat after vegetation regrowth in the Mullen Fire footprint. That supports habitat-scale scouting, not a current-location claim.
- **Principal tradeoff:** This hunt offers the longest calendar and by far the largest quota in the comparison, but its mixed ownership and 10.5-day 2025 effort warn against treating two months as an easy tag or easy pack-out.
- **Best for:** A point-invested hunter who values calendar flexibility, can build multiple legal access systems and has enough time to relocate when sign, pressure or snow changes.

## Hunt 2: Wyoming 001-4 — stronger public-land pattern, shorter clock

- **Current season and weapon:** Type 4 antlerless moose, any legal weapon, October 1–31, 2026, after a September special-archery season. A cow with a calf at side is excluded. The 2026 allocation lists 16 resident licenses and one nonresident license.
- **Tag path and 2026 draw demand:** In the resident preference pool, 12 licenses were available. The last partly successful tier was 12 points, where two of five first-choice applicants drew; applicants below 12 drew none. The resident random pool listed four licenses and 114 first choices, a raw 3.5 licenses per 100 first choices. The single nonresident license was issued in the preference pool at 23 points; the nonresident random report showed no license for four first choices.
- **Latest harvest result:** The 2025 report lists 11 hunters, eight moose, 72.7% success and 7.9 average days. With only 11 hunters, one outcome would shift success by about nine percentage points.
- **Public-land and access pattern:** The local record estimates 70% public land, the strongest ownership headline here. Hunt Area 1 sits in the Bighorn moose landscape, where broad Forest Service access can improve the starting position. It still does not establish that a road is open, a trail reaches the intended habitat or a public parcel is accessible without crossing private land.
- **Terrain character:** Think Bighorn mountain meadows, aspen-willow cover and forested drainages rather than one continuous glassing basin. Cooler feeding periods can help observation, while dense cover and steep recovery routes reward patient confirmation before a shot.
- **Principal tradeoff:** Area 1 combines a cleaner public-land pattern with a shorter October season. Its point threshold and limited random quota make the application demanding, and the October 31 close gives a current tag holder little room to rescue a failed access assumption.
- **Best for:** A hunter prioritizing public-land orientation who can scout before opening day, move deliberately through cover and protect several days for recovery before the October deadline.

## Hunt 3: Idaho 3094 — a three-tag random draw in mixed country

- **Current season and weapon:** Controlled Hunt 3094 authorizes three antlerless moose tags in all of Area 54, any weapon, October 15–November 23. Idaho's official 2025–2026 moose rules apply those dates and tag count to both years.
- **Tag path and latest displayed draw demand:** Idaho random controlled-hunt draw without preference points. The agency Hunt Planner and Hunt Planner's local structured record still display the 2025 hunt instance: 81 total first choices for three awards, rounded to 4%. The residency split was 77 resident first choices and three awards, plus four nonresident first choices and no award. The two-year regulation is the authority for the 2026 season; the profile's dated 2025 fields are the latest hunt-specific draw snapshot attached locally.
- **Latest harvest result:** Idaho's 2025 mandatory reports list three hunters and three harvested moose, or 100% success. It is the smallest possible successful three-tag sample, not evidence that a future hunter has a guaranteed result. The 2024 row was two of three.
- **Public-land and access pattern:** Idaho estimates Area 54 as 40.72% private, 34.59% Forest Service, 22.79% BLM, 1.85% state and 0.04% military management. Government management totals about 59.3%, but the agency lists no current Access Yes property. Ownership and legal access must be checked parcel by parcel.
- **Terrain character:** The agency land-cover summary is 55.03% rangeland, 23.63% irrigated land and 11.58% forest, with a small riparian share. That mix favors broad planning around forage-cover transitions and lawful public approaches rather than treating Area 54 as one uniform mountain hunt.
- **Principal tradeoff:** Idaho offers a fresh random chance with no point balance and a season extending into late November, but only three tags, a tiny harvest sample and a substantial private-land share leave little margin for casual planning.
- **Best for:** A hunter who prefers a random draw, can verify access before travel and is comfortable narrowing a very large mixed rangeland-agriculture-forest unit to a few complete habitat systems.

## A practical late-season field sequence

1. **Verify the legal animal before building tactics.** Review the state definition, then decide how long you will watch an animal or group to rule out a calf at side and confirm antler status.
2. **Build three complete access systems.** Each needs legal ingress, likely feed-cover structure, an observation route, wind options, parking and a safe extraction plan. Three glassing spots from one closed road are not three systems.
3. **Start with visibility, finish with sign.** Glass openings and edges during cooler movement periods, then use broad track and browsing patterns to decide whether nearby cover deserves a slow still-hunt. Do not publish precise camps, routes, glassing points, current animal locations or coordinates.
4. **Set a relocation rule.** Move when fresh sign is absent after a defined search, when only cows with calves are observed, or when road and weather conditions undermine safe recovery. A long season does not repay repeated days in an unproductive basin.
5. **Plan the pack before the shot.** Moose recovery is a refrigeration and labor problem. Account for distance, slope, daylight, snow, helpers, vehicle access and state carcass-transport rules before pursuing an animal away from the exit.

## Best fit by hunter priority

- **For the longest calendar and largest 2026 quota:** Wyoming 038-4, with an October 1–November 30 regular season and 35 licenses. Its 2025 hunters still averaged 10.5 days.
- **For the strongest public-land headline:** Wyoming 001-4 at an estimated 70% public. The October 31 close and access details keep that percentage from being a complete plan.
- **For a random draw without saved points:** Idaho 3094. The latest attached result was three awards from 81 first choices in 2025, and the current regulation still limits the hunt to three tags.
- **For a nonresident random path in Wyoming:** Area 38 had one 2026 nonresident random license for 12 first choices. Area 1's only nonresident license was issued in the preference pool.
- **For the smallest recent harvest sample:** Idaho 3094. Three of three in 2025 is encouraging history, but it is not stable enough to rank above the Wyoming hunts.
- **For the most calendar flexibility after a weather reset:** Wyoming 038-4. That advantage matters only if access and pack-out alternatives were built before conditions change.

There is no universal winner. Wyoming 038-4 best fits the flexible, point-invested hunter who can manage mixed access and a potentially long search. Wyoming 001-4 best fits a public-land-oriented hunter with a compressed October plan. Idaho 3094 best fits someone who values a no-points random draw and is willing to solve a large, mixed-ownership landscape for one of only three tags.

## Verification checklist

1. Confirm the hunt number, license type, legal animal, weapon, exact 2026 dates and boundary in the current state regulation and live agency hunt page.
2. Keep evidence years visible: all three seasons and Wyoming demand figures are 2026; Idaho's attached draw snapshot is 2025; all harvest results are 2025.
3. Keep Wyoming resident and nonresident preference and random pools separate. Do not apply Idaho's rounded random result to Wyoming or turn a point-tier cutoff into a future guarantee.
4. Verify the complete current license, application, point, stamp and processing cost directly with the agency for your residency before paying. This guide intentionally does not publish an incomplete bundle.
5. Recheck parcel ownership, public ingress, private permission, Access Yes enrollment, designated motorized routes, gates, fire restrictions, snow and road conditions immediately before travel.
6. Confirm tagging, evidence-of-sex, hunter-orange, carcass-transport, mandatory-reporting and disease-testing rules. Plan cooling and extraction before entering cover.
7. Carry the official boundary and current land-manager maps. Hunt Planner profiles, 3D views and the lead card are orientation tools, not field authority.
