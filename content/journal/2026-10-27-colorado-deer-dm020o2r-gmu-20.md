Colorado buck deer Hunt DM020O2R is open in GMU 20 through November 1, 2026. This brief is for a current license holder trying to use the final six calendar days, including October 27, and for a future applicant wondering why a 245-license hunt can clear every resident first choice without becoming an easy field opportunity.

The answer is access. Colorado Parks and Wildlife's current herd plan says 42% of GMU 20 is private, 22% is Rocky Mountain National Park and roughly 8% is city or county open space. Nearly one quarter is national forest, but CPW says relatively few deer remain on that ground. A zero-point draw result does not make the entire unit huntable.

## The quick verdict

DM020O2R best fits a licensed hunter who has already solved lawful access and can compare several foothill habitat systems without assuming public ownership equals deer availability. CPW's 2026 primary-draw recap shows all 166 first-choice resident adults drew. Its 2025 harvest survey estimates 68 bucks from 181 hunters, or 37.6% success.

Those figures describe different parts of the decision. The draw record says the license did not exhaust its adult resident allocation on first choice. The harvest estimate says fewer than two in five hunters filled under 2025 conditions. Neither statistic identifies where deer are today.

## What the 2026 license covers

The 2026 Colorado Big Game brochure lists `D-M-020-O2-R` as a limited buck deer rifle license valid in GMU 20 from October 24 through November 1. It is not the same license as the November 7-15 third-rifle code, the November 18-22 fourth-rifle code or the separate private-land-only season.

The primary-draw recap lists 245 total licenses and no remaining balance after the draw. The post-draw distribution included 186 resident adults, 14 nonresident adults, 32 resident youths, two nonresident youths and 11 unrestricted landowner-preference licenses. A current holder should read the physical license and current brochure together; a person without a license should not treat the accessible draw as evidence that inventory is available now.

## Every resident first choice drew, then the draw kept going

CPW recorded 166 first-choice resident adult applicants, and all 166 drew. Thirteen of 14 first-choice nonresident adults drew. First-choice resident applicants at every reported point tier, including 123 at zero points, were successful.

The remaining general allocation reached second choices. At that final adult level, 20 of 182 resident applicants and one of 11 nonresident applicants drew. That sequence is why “choice two” is more accurate than calling the hunt a 100% draw across all paths. It also explains why dividing 245 licenses by 180 adult first-choice applicants would produce a false odds estimate: youth and landowner pools also received licenses, and second-choice demand absorbed the balance.

For a future applicant, the useful 2026 fact is that no resident adult needed to spend a first-choice preference advantage. It is still one historical draw. CPW warns that quotas, codes and applicant pools can change.

## The 2025 hunt-code estimate was 37.6%

CPW's 2025 deer harvest report estimates that 181 DM020O2R hunters took 68 bucks over 728 recreation days. That is 37.6% success and about four reported recreation days per hunter. These are survey estimates, not a complete count, and they belong to last year's weather, pressure, access and deer distribution.

The data years matter. The 245-license quota and applicant sequence are from 2026. The hunt-specific harvest is from 2025. The herd plan's latest modeled population is from 2024. Keeping those years separate prevents a tidy but unsupported claim that this season will reproduce last year's result.

## GMU 20 is one boundary with several access regimes

CPW's September 2025 Big Thompson herd plan treats DAU D-10 and GMU 20 as the same 1,206-square-mile area. It describes 42% private land, nearly 25% Arapaho and Roosevelt National Forest, 22% Rocky Mountain National Park, roughly 8% city and county open space and less than 1% CPW ownership.

Those shares are not interchangeable. Hunting is prohibited in Rocky Mountain National Park. City and county open spaces have property-specific rules. Private land requires permission. A national-forest polygon still needs lawful ingress and a currently designated motorized route. The 2025 Forest Service motor-vehicle-use map also warns that a designated route is not a promise that it is maintained, passable or safe.

CPW says the herd has shifted toward places where hunting does not occur, including the national park, open space and some private land. It also says relatively few deer remain on national-forest ground. That agency conclusion is more useful than treating the unit's total public-colored acreage as a quality score.

## Build a six-day plan around boundaries and current sign

- **Separate ownership from opportunity.** Mark the park boundary, open-space rules, private parcels and legal access before choosing a glassing area. Do not use a public patch that depends on an unverified private crossing.
- **Work the elevation transition as a pattern.** The herd plan describes alpine and subalpine country in the west, ponderosa pine, aspen, meadows and riparian cover through the middle, and foothill shrublands and plains to the east. It says deer move toward winter range below 8,000 feet as snow accumulates. Compare legal feed-cover systems across that transition; do not convert the elevation description into a promised waypoint.
- **Let current evidence beat the map.** Tracks, fresh browsing, direct observations, weather and hunting pressure should decide whether to stay. An attractive national-forest basin can be biologically plausible and still hold few deer this week.
- **Keep recovery lawful.** Plan tagging, evidence of sex, meat cooling and the complete retrieval path before a shot. If a wounded animal enters Rocky Mountain National Park, do not pursue or shoot it there; contact park staff.
- **Make a CWD choice in advance.** The herd plan reports 8% prevalence among tested adult male mule deer in D-10 in 2022. CPW requires no mandatory deer testing in 2026, but voluntary testing is available for $25. Review sampling and carcass-transport rules before leaving the field.

This article deliberately stops at habitat and access patterns. It publishes no camps, private approaches, migration crossings, glassing points or animal coordinates.

## Verification checklist

1. Match the physical license to `DM020O2R`, buck deer, GMU 20 and October 24-November 1, 2026.
2. Recheck the current brochure for legal methods, fluorescent orange or pink, shooting hours, tagging and evidence-of-sex rules.
3. Verify the unit boundary, every parcel edge, permission, road, gate and temporary closure with CPW and the responsible land manager.
4. Treat Rocky Mountain National Park as closed to hunting and contact a ranger before any wounded-animal retrieval into the park.
5. Keep the 2026 draw, 2025 harvest estimate, 2024 herd estimate and 2022 CWD sample in their correct years.
6. Treat historical draw and harvest results as planning evidence, never a guarantee.
