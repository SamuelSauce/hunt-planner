Wyoming elk Hunt 124-1 opens its any-legal-weapon season today, October 15, 2026. The final regulation authorizes 100 any-elk licenses through November 30. Demand moved the other direction: Wyoming Game and Fish Department's 2026 resident report lists 1,271 first-choice applicants for 57 licenses allocated to that drawing, up from 1,133 first choices for 60 resident draw licenses in the local 2025 record.

The latest completed harvest is just as conspicuous. WGFD reports 87 elk harvested and 83.7% success in 2025. Yet its Hunt Planner also labels public access “Good,” displays a “Difficult Public Access” warning and describes a true private-public checkerboard across the northern half. Hunt 124-1 is therefore a useful case in reading all the evidence together: scarce tag, strong historical harvest and 70% public land do not eliminate the need for a legally connected access plan.

Start with the [static Hunt Planner profile for Hunt 124-1](/hunts/wyoming/elk/124-1-hunt-area-124/) and [open Area 124 in the Hunt Planner 3D map](/?state=WY&hunt=wy-elk-124-1-0&residency=resident&view=3d). The card above uses the actual Powder Rim hunt boundary. It is an orientation graphic, not an ownership, road-status or navigation map.

## The quick verdict

Hunt 124-1 best fits a tag holder prepared to search a large, dry sagebrush system as water, forage, pressure or legal access changes elk distribution. The 47-day regular season gives time to reset, but the 2025 report's 12 days per hunter is a better workload signal than the 83.7% headline. Verify several routes and let current sign overrule a block's reputation.

For a future applicant, the 2026 resident pool became materially tighter. Nonresidents faced separate regular and special preference pools, and neither random pool issued a 124-1 license. A single blended “Wyoming draw odds” number would hide the actual system.

## What the 2026 license authorizes

The final 2026 Chapter 7 regulation lists Area 124 Type 1 as an any-elk license with a quota of 100. Its special-archery season ran September 1–30; the regular season runs October 15–November 30. The quota is unchanged from the final 2025 season listed in WGFD's latest Petition herd report.

“Any elk” matters when interpreting the harvest result. The 83.7% figure covers the Type 1 license, not bull-only success. A hunter focused only on a mature bull cannot treat area-wide success as an 83.7% personal forecast.

Area 124 also contains Type 2, 4 and 7 seasons with different dates, animal limitations and geographic restrictions. A Type 1 holder should not borrow another license type's later window or subarea language.

## Resident demand rose while draw allocation fell

The June 15, 2026 resident demand report lists 57 licenses and 1,271 first-choice applicants for 124-1. That is a raw quota-to-first-choice ratio of 4.48%. The local 2025 record lists 60 resident draw licenses and 1,133 applicants, or 5.30%.

First-choice demand increased by 138 applicants, or 12.2%, while the reported resident allocation declined by three. The raw ratio fell about 0.82 percentage point. These completed-draw summaries do not promise the next result; party applications, allocations and applicant behavior can change it.

The 100-license regulation quota and the 57-license resident report answer different questions. The first is the hunt's total authorized quota; the second is the quantity shown in that particular drawing report. They should not be presented as conflicting or interchangeable totals.

## Nonresident random pools issued zero tags

The 2026 regular preference report shows three 124-1 licenses. All three went at 20 points, where 19 first-choice applicants produced a reported 15.79% success rate; the report shows none below 20. The special preference report shows two licenses, also at 20 points, with two issued among 13 first-choice applicants for 15.38%; none went below 20.

Both nonresident random reports list zero licenses for this hunt. The regular random pool still recorded 443 first choices, and the special random pool recorded 66. That does not mean applicants had tiny positive odds—it means those two 2026 random pools offered no 124-1 licenses at all.

The special application changes price and applicant pool, not the hunt boundary or season. Keep regular preference, special preference, regular random and special random results separate, and do not treat a historical point tier as a reservation for a future draw.

## Read 83.7% beside 12 days per hunter

WGFD's 2025 Hunt Planner report lists 1,045 hunter-days, 12 days per hunter, 87 elk harvested and 83.7% success for Type 1. Hunt Planner's local structured record identifies 104 hunters afield. These are strong historical results, but they do not separate bulls from cows, public from private land, archery from the regular season or one part of Area 124 from another.

Twelve days per hunter is a substantial commitment. High area-wide success can coexist with long searches, access differences and elk moving between huntable and unhuntable ground.

WGFD's latest published Petition herd report, covering June 2024 through May 2025, helps explain why managers raised the Type 1 quota from 70 to 100 for the 2025 season. The agency cited high success and satisfaction and reported a 2024 average harvested-bull age of 6.5 years from 22 samples, with a three-year average of 6.8. It explicitly warned that low and biased tooth-sample participation made the absolute average problematic. Those are older, sample-limited management indicators—not a 2026 trophy forecast.

The 2026 Green River forecast says most elk herds are at or above objective and anticipates generally good opportunity. It gives no Area 124 bull-age or success prediction, so use it only as regional context.

## Seventy percent public is not seventy percent simple

WGFD estimates Area 124 at 70% public land and rates access “Good.” The detail matters more than either label. The agency describes excellent public access in the southern half, excluding the Little Snake River corridor, while calling the northern half a true checkerboard with unique access complexities. Its page simultaneously carries a difficult-public-access warning.

The landscape is semi-arid sagebrush plain. Broken badlands along the southern and western edges add juniper, mountain mahogany, saltbush and concealment. WGFD says water can be scarce and that elk distribution depends heavily on resource availability, with cover often limited to taller vegetation, folds and gullies. That supports a habitat-pattern plan—not publication of springs, camps, routes, glassing points or animal coordinates.

The Hunt Planner points hunters to the Tipton Hunter Management Area on the northern periphery. That property requires the appropriate permission slip and vehicle pass. Its online table still displays an August 5, 2025 results date for the Area 124 elk drawing, so do not assume that row establishes 2026 availability. Verify the current permission status and ranch rules directly before relying on it.

## A field plan for the opener

- **Prove the complete route.** Confirm parcel status, legal entry, road authority, parking, foot travel and retrieval before committing to a public block. Public acreage beyond an unlawful private crossing is not practical access.
- **Search resources at landscape scale.** Compare broad moisture, forage and security-cover patterns, then update with fresh sign and direct observations. Do not circulate precise water or animal locations.
- **Budget for historical effort.** The 2025 report's 12 days per hunter argues for time, fuel, water, tire repair, meat care and recovery capacity.
- **Build a checkerboard alternative.** Have a southern public-land plan and at least one legally verified backup rather than waiting on an uncertain northern crossing.
- **Check live conditions.** Drought, rain on clay soils, fire restrictions and road closures can change both safe travel and elk distribution. Recheck agency and land-manager information immediately before entry.
- **Read harvest rules before the shot.** Confirm tagging, evidence-of-sex, transport and chronic-wasting-disease guidance while service is available.

Hunt 124-1 is attractive for real reasons: a long season, any-elk authority, a public-land majority and strong recent harvest. Its central tradeoff is equally real. The 2026 draw was tighter, the 2025 effort was substantial and the access picture changes sharply across the area. Treat the big numbers as planning evidence, not permission to skip the ownership map or the current sign.
