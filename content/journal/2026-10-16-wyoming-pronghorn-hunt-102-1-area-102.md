Wyoming pronghorn Hunt 102-1 opened to any legal weapon October 15, 2026, after a two-month special-archery period. The final regulation authorizes 300 any-antelope licenses, and the resident drawing offered 240 licenses against only 135 first-choice applicants. That looks like abundant opportunity. The field evidence is harder: Wyoming Game and Fish Department estimates just 20% public land, flags difficult public access and reported 61.5% success across 262 active hunters in 2025.

The lesson is not that 102-1 is easy to draw or hard to hunt in every circumstance. It is that application availability and practical access answer different questions. This hunt best fits a license holder who has already proved several legal entry systems—or secured written private access—and can stay after opening pressure fades.

## What the 2026 license authorizes

WGFD lists 102-1 as a limited-quota Type 1 license valid for any antelope. The special-archery season ran August 15 through October 14, and the regular season runs October 15 through November 15, 2026. The final Chapter 5 table sets a total quota of 300 and marks the license with the difficult-public-access asterisk.

That 300-license season quota is not the resident first-choice supply. Resident, nonresident, landowner, preference, random, regular and special allocations follow separate rules. A tag holder should also distinguish 102-1 from the Area 102 Type 6 doe/fawn licenses, which carry different limitations and dates.

## Resident availability did not remove the access problem

The June 15, 2026 resident demand report lists 240 draw licenses, 135 first-choice applicants, 126 second choices and 66 third choices for 102-1. First-choice demand used only 56.3% of that resident draw inventory. In the comparable 2025 report, 126 first-choice applicants sought 239 licenses.

Those rows show that resident first-choice demand was below resident supply in two completed drawings. They do **not** promise a leftover license, because lower choices, party processing, landowner allocations and subsequent sale rules still matter. They also say nothing about whether an applicant has public access that works on the ground.

Nonresident results move in the opposite direction and must stay separate. In 2026, the regular random report lists 11 licenses against 162 first-choice applicants, while the special random report lists seven against 33. Preference licenses were allocated through their own point-tier tables. The special license changes price and draw pool; it does not improve public access inside Area 102.

## What 61.5% success actually describes

WGFD's 2025 harvest report lists 262 active Type 1 hunters, 161 antelope harvested and 1,302 hunter days. That equals 61.5% success and 8.1 days per active hunter. The local Hunt Planner profile correctly carries those completed-season figures forward as historical evidence.

The result is not a firearm-only success rate, a public-land success rate or an individual forecast. It blends hunters with different access, timing and effort across the full license. The 8.1-day average is especially useful: even successful outcomes can require substantially more time than the open-country visibility of pronghorn suggests.

Keep the years explicit. Season structure, quota and demand are from 2026; harvest performance is from 2025. Combining them into one score would hide the access and effort questions a current hunter actually needs to solve.

## Twenty percent public, with concentrated pressure

The current WGFD Hunt Planner estimates Area 102 at 20% public land, rates public access “Fair” and warns that limited public lands receive high hunting pressure. It describes broken foothills along the Bighorn Mountains grading into gentler grassland, sagebrush-grassland and irrigated agricultural country.

The agency says moderate pronghorn densities occupy the area, but more animals move onto private land once hunting begins. It also expects the highest hunter densities during the first few days and says later hunters should encounter fewer people. That is a timing observation, not assurance that animals will remain on reachable parcels.

The page identifies the Bud Love Wildlife Management Area, Wyoming State Trust lands and several Access Yes properties as possible pieces of the access puzzle. Each still needs a current species check, season check, permission rule and legal route. State trust land is not a substitute for verifying the road used to reach it.

One timely trap deserves emphasis: the Fort Phil Kearny Hunter Management Area lists Area 102 antelope access only from September 1 through October 14 and specifies archery equipment. Its antelope window ended before the regular season opened. A permission slip from that earlier period is not evidence of any-legal-weapon access on October 15.

WGFD's 2026 Sheridan Region forecast adds broader context. It says regional pronghorn herds have begun trending upward after declines tied to drought, disease and hard winters, while moderate-to-extreme 2026 drought could shift animals toward irrigated forage. The same forecast warns of difficult private access and crowding on accessible public ground. Those are regional signals, not an Area 102 population estimate.

## A practical plan after the opener

- **Audit access before animals.** Confirm ownership, the public road or easement, parking authority, walk-in boundary, permission dates and retrieval path for every option.
- **Separate regular-season access from archery access.** Recheck every HMA or Walk-In Area after October 14; do not assume its dates match the hunt license.
- **Build several independent systems.** A public parcel, a current Access Yes property and written private permission should be treated as different plans, each with its own rules.
- **Use timing deliberately.** WGFD expects opening pressure on accessible land. If schedule allows, compare verified midweek and later-season options rather than forcing an uncertain crossing.
- **Scout habitat patterns, not pins.** Observe broad transitions among grassland, sagebrush, broken foothills and irrigated ground, then let current sign and legal access determine the next move.
- **Keep sensitive details private.** Do not publish water sources, camps, private approaches, glassing points, current animal locations or coordinates.

## Verification checklist

- Match the license to Area 102, Type 1 and the any-antelope limitation.
- Confirm the October 15–November 15 regular season in the final 2026 rules.
- Verify every parcel, road, HMA, Walk-In Area and permission slip against current official maps and property rules.
- Recheck weather, fire restrictions, road conditions and any emergency agency notices immediately before entry.
- Carry required licenses and permissions and review tagging, evidence-of-sex and transport rules before hunting.
- Keep 2026 quota and draw evidence separate from the 2025 harvest report.

Hunt 102-1 is a useful warning against equating tag availability with easy opportunity. Resident first-choice demand was light, but public acreage is limited, opening pressure is concentrated and recent hunters averaged more than eight days afield. The best fit is a hunter who values a long regular season and has already solved access—not someone hoping the 300-license headline will solve it on arrival.
