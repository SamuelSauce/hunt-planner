Colorado doe deer Hunt DF444O3R opens in GMU 444 on November 7, 2026. This brief is for a tag holder using the final four days to turn a small antlerless license into a lawful access plan, and for a future applicant deciding what a one-point draw and a 20% harvest headline actually mean. Both numbers need context.

The card uses the actual GMU 444 polygon. It does not establish public access, an open road or deer on a particular slope.

## The quick verdict

DF444O3R best fits a current tag holder who can verify several legal entry systems before the opener and let fresh sign replace a rigid preseason plan. Colorado Parks and Wildlife offered only 10 licenses in the 2026 primary draw. Twenty people applied first choice, and all 10 successful applicants came from that first-choice pool.

The latest field result is much less precise than that draw record. CPW's 2025 harvest survey estimates two does from 10 hunters, or 20% success, and marks the row with its low-response asterisk. That is useful evidence against assuming an easy antlerless hunt, but it is too small and survey-dependent to forecast one hunter's 2026 result.

## What the 2026 license covers

The current 2026 Big Game brochure lists `D-F-444-O3-R` as a List B doe deer license valid in GMU 444 from November 7 through November 15. The code is not the GMU 444 buck license, and it is not the separate private-land either-sex code. Match the physical license to the sex, unit and dates before leaving home.

Third rifle also puts deer, elk and bear hunters in the field at the same time. Orange or pink, shooting-hour, tagging, evidence-of-sex and carcass-transport rules apply independently of how small the quota is. A 10-license deer hunt is not a 10-person landscape.

## Ten of 20 first choices drew

CPW's 2026 recap shows a total quota of 10 and zero licenses remaining after the draw. The first-choice pool contained 20 people: 13 resident adults, five resident youths and two nonresident youths. The successful distribution was five resident adults, four resident youths and one nonresident youth. No nonresident adult applied first choice.

The resident-adult draw reached one preference point, where three of three applicants at the final level drew. That does not mean every one-point applicant will draw in 2027. The quota, youth allocation, applicant mix and point distribution can all change, and Colorado awards preference points through first-choice outcomes rather than later choices.

Ten divided by 20 is a useful overall first-choice result for this one draw, not a universal 50% odds promise. The cohort details matter: half of the successful licenses went to youths, while eight resident adults at zero points were unsuccessful.

## The 2025 harvest estimate was 20%

CPW's 2025 report estimates two does harvested by 10 DF444O3R hunters over 33 recreation days. That equals 20% success and 3.3 recreation days per hunter. The report places an asterisk beside this hunt code because few hunters responded under CPW's published response-rate rule.

The survey is voluntary and sample-based. CPW extrapolates participation and harvest from responses, then identifies rows with especially limited response. The two-deer estimate is not a complete check-in count, and 2025 weather, access, pressure and animal distribution do not describe November 2026. Publish the limitation with the number; do not hide it behind a clean percentage.

## Herd scale is not hunt scale

CPW manages GMU 444 as the Basalt deer herd, Data Analysis Unit D-53. Its 2025 population table estimates 3,590 post-hunt deer and 25 bucks per 100 does. The agency's Northwest Region herd-plan package retains objectives of 4,000–6,000 deer and 32–40 bucks per 100 does for D-53.

Those are herd-wide model estimates and objectives, not counts of legal does available to DF444O3R hunters. They also do not explain the 10-license quota by themselves. Use them to understand the management scale, then return to the exact hunt code, current boundary and fresh field evidence.

The same planning material emphasizes how snow, forage and development shape western Colorado mule deer range. For a November hunt, compare broad lower-elevation browse, open feeding edges and nearby security cover without assuming every deer has already moved. Let tracks, droppings and direct observations decide whether a habitat pattern is active.

## Access is the first scouting problem

GMU 444 contains a mix of public and private land, and a colored public parcel does not prove legal ingress. The Colorado Hunting Atlas is the agency starting point for unit, ownership and seasonal-range layers. On National Forest land, the current Motor Vehicle Use Map controls designated motorized routes and seasonal dates; a mapped road is not automatically open or passable.

Basalt State Wildlife Area provides one concrete public-land example inside GMUs 47 and 444. CPW lists 2,577 acres, deer hunting and elevations from roughly 6,514 to 8,777 feet, but the property has its own access, camping and discharge rules. Verify the current map and regulations rather than extending those rules to the rest of GMU 444.

Build two independent legal plans before the opener:

- **Audit the whole chain.** Confirm the GMU line, parcel, public ingress, parking, route designation and retrieval path. Private land need not be posted, and permission is required to enter it for recovery.
- **Separate habitat from access.** A promising browse-and-cover system is not useful until every step from parking to pack-out is lawful.
- **Update with current sign.** Test several broad elevation and cover bands. Do not force a high-country assumption or a winter-range assumption onto quiet ground.
- **Plan for shared pressure.** Expect elk, buck deer and bear hunters, give others room and keep a safe shooting background.
- **Prepare the recovery.** Decide how to tag, preserve evidence of sex, cool meat and handle a boundary-crossing animal before taking a shot.

Keep all shared guidance at the habitat-pattern level. Do not publish camps, private approaches, bedding cover, crossings, glassing points or animal coordinates.
