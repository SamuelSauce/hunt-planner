Wyoming deer Hunt 141-1 changes character today, October 22, 2026. The license remains valid through October 31 for an antlered mule deer or any white-tailed deer, but only on national forest. Land elsewhere in Hunt Area 141 that was legal through October 21 is no longer included in the regular-season authorization.

That rule change arrives three days after the Wyoming Game and Fish Department announced the first confirmed chronic wasting disease detection in Deer Hunt Area 141. The finding does not create a new season or prove an area-wide prevalence rate. It does add a current testing and carcass-handling decision to a hunt already defined by a midseason land-status change.

It does not shade the national-forest-only portion that becomes controlling today, so verify the current land layer and legal boundary before entering the field.

## The quick verdict

Hunt 141-1 now best fits a tag holder who can build a complete plan on verified national forest, handle steep late-October country and adapt to deer moving between alpine, timber, aspen and lower open habitat. The 2025 mule deer report's 70% success is encouraging, but its 10.1 hunter-days per harvested buck is the better workload signal.

The hunt is a poor fit for someone relying on yesterday's pin, treating every public parcel as legal after October 21 or assuming the new CWD detection predicts the status of an individual deer. Recheck the land manager, carry a testing plan and keep multiple habitat-scale options.

## What changes on October 22

The final 2026 Chapter 6 regulation gives Hunt Area 141 Type 1 two consecutive regular-season lines. From October 1–21, the license was valid for an antlered mule deer or any white-tailed deer across the hunt area, subject to all other rules and lawful access. From October 22–31, the same animal limitation applies **on national forest only**. The total 2026 quota is 75.

“National forest only” is a legal limitation, not a suggestion about where deer should be. Bureau of Land Management, state and private parcels inside the Area 141 outline do not become legal merely because the license is still open. Nor does a Forest Service destination make the route lawful: confirm the complete access chain, current road or trail status, parking and retrieval authority.

WGFD estimates the full area at 80% public land and rates public access “Good,” yet it also marks the hunt with its difficult-public-access asterisk. Those labels can coexist. The public-land percentage describes the whole hunt area; today's rule is narrower, and much of the high country carries physical or wilderness constraints.

## Resident demand reached 1,056 first choices

The June 15, 2026 resident demand report lists 58 drawing licenses and 1,056 first-choice applicants for 141-1. That is a raw quota-to-first-choice ratio of 5.49%.

The local 2025 record lists 38 resident drawing licenses and 852 first choices, or 4.46%. First-choice demand increased by 204 applications, or 23.9%, while the resident report's license count rose by 20. The raw ratio improved by about 1.03 percentage points because the reported allocation grew faster than demand.

The 75-license season quota and 58 resident drawing licenses are not competing totals. The first covers the full regulated quota; the second is the quantity shown in the resident report. Landowner and nonresident allocations are handled separately.

Nonresidents also need four distinct results, not one blended “Wyoming odds” number. In 2026, the regular preference report issued the final licenses at 18 points and shows none below 18. The special preference report's last partial tier was 16 points at 50%. The regular random report lists one license against 103 first choices, while the special random report lists one against 53. The special license changes price and applicant pool, not the October 22 land restriction.

## Read 70% success beside 352 hunter-days

WGFD's 2025 mule deer table estimates 50 active Type 1 hunters, 35 bucks harvested and 352 hunter-days. That is 70% success and 10.1 days per harvested deer. The resident estimate was 24 bucks for 38 active hunters, or 63.2%; the nonresident estimate was 11 for 12, or 91.7%.

Those are survey estimates from one completed season, not a 2026 forecast. The table does not separate harvest before and after the October 22 restriction, national forest from other ownership, wilderness from non-wilderness or one habitat band from another. It also reports mule deer, not a combined mule deer and white-tailed result. The most defensible planning lesson is that strong area-wide success still came with substantial effort.

WGFD's August 31, 2026 forecast says the Sublette herd is in an upswing and likely reaching its population objective this year after several harsh winters complicated recovery. That is herd-level context. It does not guarantee a buck in Area 141 or erase the effect of weather, migration timing, hunting pressure and the national-forest-only rule.

## The terrain and access test

Area 141 rises from sagebrush flats and open parks along the upper Green River into aspen, conifer forest, alpine habitat and rugged rock in the Wind River and Gros Ventre ranges. WGFD describes deer distribution as patchy and says fall movement toward winter range typically begins in mid-October. Treat that as a landscape model, not a route prescription: compare broad elevation, forage, security cover and migration-stage patterns, then let current tracks, droppings and sightings update the plan.

Approximately 37.31% of Area 141 is designated wilderness. Wyoming requires a nonresident hunting big or trophy game inside designated wilderness to be accompanied by a licensed outfitter or qualified resident guide. That requirement applies within the wilderness boundary, not across all of Area 141. Residents still need to account for wilderness travel rules, remoteness, weather and retrieval time.

Grizzly bears are relatively common in the area. WGFD recommends conflict precautions, including carrying bear spray. Build meat care and retrieval around the possibility of a long pack-out, and follow current food-storage and land-manager rules.

## A field plan for the final ten days

- **Rebuild the map for today's rule.** Remove every plan that depends on non-Forest-Service ground, then verify the remaining parcel, route, parking and retrieval chain with current agency and land-manager information.
- **Keep several elevation bands in play.** Mid-October movement is not a promise that every deer has migrated. Compare broad alpine-edge, timber-aspen and forest-to-open-park systems without publishing beds, camps, crossings, glassing points, routes or animal coordinates.
- **Budget for the historical effort.** The 2025 report's 10.1 days per harvested buck argues for time, weather margins, fuel, warm clothing, meat care and a realistic recovery plan.
- **Separate wilderness from national forest.** All wilderness here is national forest, but not all national forest is designated wilderness. Nonresidents must know exactly where the guide requirement begins.
- **Plan for CWD before the shot.** Decide where to submit a sample and how to comply with current carcass-transport and disposal rules while cell service and daylight are available.
- **Carry bear conflict tools and knowledge.** Keep attractants controlled, make the recovery plan bear-aware and follow current Forest Service and WGFD guidance.

## The new CWD finding

On October 19, 2026, WGFD confirmed CWD in Deer Hunt Area 141 for the first time. The agency notes that neighboring Areas 128, 142, 154 and 155 had earlier detections. Presence is not prevalence: the announcement does not publish a percentage for Area 141 and should not be converted into one.

WGFD encourages hunters to submit samples for free testing and asks the public to report deer that appear sick or injured. The Pinedale Region also publishes current drop-off options. Follow the current agency instructions for sample preparation, results, carcass transport and approved disposal; other states may impose additional import rules.

Hunt 141-1 remains an attractive limited-quota mule deer opportunity, but the final ten days are not simply an extension of opening week. The legal footprint narrows today, current disease information changed this week and the latest draw became more competitive even as the resident allocation grew. A good plan begins by treating all three facts as current—not as footnotes to a 70% historical success rate.
